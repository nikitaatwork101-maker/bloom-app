See how it works

People let relationships fade not because they stop caring — but because there's no visible sign anything is wrong, until it already is.

A plant on your desk wilts where you can see it. A friendship doesn't. Bloom gives it the same warning a leaf gives you.

How a Bloom grows

Three habits, each small enough to do in a few taps.

Plant

Add a Bloom for anyone who matters

Family, an old roommate, someone you only see once a year — each one gets its own flower the moment you add them.

Log

A message, a call, a video chat

Logging contact takes a few taps — pick the type, rate how full your cup felt from barely to overflowing, done.

Tend

Watch the health score do the remembering

Contact and quiet time both move the score in real time. A daily prompt points you toward whoever needs you most today.

Built for how relationships actually work

Not every Bloom needs the same amount of water.

Cadence that fits the relationship

Daily, weekly, every two weeks, or a number you set yourself — decay reads from what's normal for that person, not one rule stretched across everyone in your garden.

Prompts that know the difference

Same city gets "grab coffee this week." Long distance gets "send a voice memo." The daily nudge changes its suggestion based on where you both actually are.

A garden, not a list

Group Blooms into Family, Friends, or tabs you make yourself. Sorted so whoever's health score is lowest — and needs you most — rises to the top.

Everything, without the noise

A notification bell keeps a quiet, in-app record of prompts and events — no push permissions, no browser pop-ups you never asked for.

Backed by research

Strong relationships predict how long you live — an effect that rivals quitting smoking.

A meta-analysis of 148 studies and over 300,000 people found that people with stronger social relationships were 50% more likely to still be alive years later than those without — a survival effect on par with quitting smoking, and bigger than exercising regularly. So why not spend five minutes today checking in on someone you love? That's how a village gets built: a little, often, not all at once.

Holt-Lunstad, J., Smith, T. B., & Layton, J. B. (2010). Social Relationships and Mortality Risk: A Meta-analytic Review. PLoS Medicine, 7(7), e1000316.
